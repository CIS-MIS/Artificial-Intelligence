# Homework 01: Intelligent Agents and Task Environments

**Chapters:** 1 (Introduction to AI) and 2 (Intelligent Agents)
**Estimated time:** 2–3 hours

## Instructions

Answer all questions thoroughly. Where a question asks you to apply the PEAS framework or analyze an agent, be explicit about each component and justify your design choices. Where a calculation is required, show your work. Submissions should be typed and well organized, with question numbers clearly labeled.

## Question 1: What Is AI? (15 points)

(a) Distinguish artificial intelligence from ordinary software. In your answer, identify at least three characteristics that make a system "intelligent" in the AI sense rather than merely programmed. (5 points)

(b) The Turing Test proposes an operational criterion for machine intelligence. Describe the setup of the imitation game and explain what passing the test would (and would not) demonstrate about a machine. (5 points)

(c) Give one example of a task that current AI systems perform well (narrow AI) and one capability that would require artificial general intelligence (AGI) but does not yet exist. Explain the difference. (5 points)

---

## Question 2: PEAS Framework (20 points)

For each of the following agent types, fill out a PEAS description. Be specific and realistic.

(a) A robotic vacuum cleaner that operates in a single-family home. (5 points)

(b) An automated stock-trading agent that buys and sells equities on behalf of a client. (5 points)

(c) An AI tutor that helps high-school students learn algebra. (5 points)

(d) A spam-filtering agent for an email service. (5 points)

For each, list: Performance measure, Environment, Actuators, Sensors.

## Question 3: Rationality vs. Omniscience (15 points)

(a) Define rationality for an agent. How does rationality differ from omniscience? (7 points)

(b) Consider a self-driving car approaching an intersection with a partially occluded view of crossing traffic. The car proceeds through the intersection because its sensors do not detect any vehicle, but a car hidden behind a building is actually approaching quickly, resulting in a collision. Was the self-driving car's decision rational? Was it omniscient? Justify your answer using the definitions from part (a). (8 points)

## Question 4: Agent Architectures (20 points)

(a) Describe the four agent architectures discussed in Chapter 2: simple reflex, model-based reflex, goal-based, and utility-based. For each, state what information the agent maintains internally and how it selects actions. (12 points)

(b) For each of the following scenarios, identify which architecture is most appropriate and explain why:

- (i) A thermostat that turns the heat on when temperature falls below a setpoint. (2 points)
- (ii) A GPS navigation system that computes the fastest route to a destination. (2 points)
- (iii) A package-delivery drone that balances delivery speed against battery consumption and risk of package damage. (2 points)
- (iv) An autonomous car that must decide whether to brake or swerve when a pedestrian suddenly appears. (2 points)

## Question 5: Task Environment Properties (15 points)

Classify each of the following environments along the five dimensions discussed in Chapter 2: observable vs. partially observable, deterministic vs. stochastic, static vs. dynamic, discrete vs. continuous, and single-agent vs. multi-agent. Provide a one-sentence justification for each classification.

(a) Playing chess against a computer opponent. (3 points)

(b) Driving through city traffic during rush hour. (3 points)

(c) Solving a crossword puzzle alone. (3 points)

(d) Playing poker with three human opponents. (3 points)

(e) A factory robot assembling parts on a fixed conveyor belt. (3 points)

## Question 6: The Perception-Deliberation-Action Loop (15 points)

Draw or describe the perception-deliberation-action loop for an intelligent agent. Then, using the example of a robot delivering a package across a campus, explain what happens at each stage of the loop. Include at least one way in which the agent updates its internal model between sensing and acting, and one way in which the environment may change while the agent is deliberating. (15 points)
